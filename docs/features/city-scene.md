# F02 · City scene

Last updated: 2026-10-13 · Status: v0 (art passes 1 to 4 done) · Launch blocker: real-phone performance, final polish

## What it does
- A miniature San Francisco in three.js (React Three Fiber).
- Time of day drives the sun, sky, fog, window lights, street lamps and bridge lights.
- Hills: Russian Hill (under the 06:00 rooftop), Nob Hill, Telegraph Hill, Twin Peaks (two peaks), Pacific Heights. Streets climb them straight up, never steeper than 0.36.
- Parks: Golden Gate Park, Twin Peaks, Pioneer Park on Telegraph Hill (Coit Tower), Dolores Park. Grass only grows in parks.
- Street grid with sidewalks and parks. Pastel Victorian row houses with real 3D bay windows, cornices and brackets, hoods, sills, entries and stoops that line up with their facade photos; gable roofs. Downtown and SoMa towers in glass or stone.
- Life: 13 Teslas (9 sedans, 2 Cybertrucks, 2 Roadsters): 7 on two city block loops, 6 crossing the Golden Gate and the Bay Bridge, sailboats with wakes and a ferry on the bay, Alcatraz with its lighthouse.
- Landmarks: Russian Hill rooftop, Hayes Valley shop, Mission wall, Union Square billboard, Transamerica Pyramid, Coit Tower, Ferry Building, California St and Union Square cable cars, Embarcadero pier, Bay Bridge, Golden Gate, and a fog bank off the ocean.

## How it works
- `src/lib/terrain.ts`: `heightAt(x, z)`, a sum of smooth elliptical domes that are exactly flat outside their radius. Everything on the ground uses it, including chapter spots (`at()` in `day.ts`). Hill sizes keep every street under `MAX_STREET_GRADE` (tested); the steep tops sit in parks.
- `grid.ts`: streets, `PARKS` (whole blocks) and `groundAt(x, z)` (street, sidewalk, grass or block). A park closes the streets inside it, so a street that reaches a park ends at a T junction with a kerb. The ground texture paints exactly `groundAt`.
- `layout.ts`: pure and seeded. Blocks, houses, towers, trees, lamps. Buildings sit level with their highest corner (nothing sunk); the footing covers the drop on the downhill side. It keeps streets, landmarks (`CLEAR`) and every camera's line of sight (`blocksView`) free. Nothing near the pyramid grows taller than it.
- `houses.tsx`: the row houses. A box with one of six real Painted Lady fronts (atlas tiles, `FACADES` measures each photo: bay, cornice, brackets, ledges, posts, entry, steps, windows) and in front of it a relief built from that table, one instanced mesh per tile. Photos are never stretched: a house's height is its body width times the photo's aspect (`FACADE_ASPECT` in layout.ts, so houses are 3.8 to 5.0 tall; room in the row is still checked at the old storey heights, so the row is unchanged). Faces that look out take the photo by front projection, so a bay's front shows the bay's front and its angled sides the side windows (unfolded, at most 1.7x); sides, tops and undersides of parts are one flat trim colour (`FLAT`, the photo there blurred), never a smeared strip. Tests check square texels on every house and the apartment, and the stretch on every relief face. Night: each window of the table has an id (`windowIds`, a small data texture), and about half light up as whole windows, bay faces included. The relief stands out up to `RELIEF_DEPTH` (0.36, the cornice crown); on the 26 house fronts next to Cuso's walking lines it is flattened to 0.13 (`reliefDepth`) so his arms clear it. The collision test uses `reliefDepth`.
- `materials.ts`: textured materials (shader patches on `MeshStandardMaterial`). `houseMaterial` maps the atlas tile on the house front (and on the relief by its `aUv`) and tinted lap siding elsewhere; towers repeat glass or limestone facades in world units; roofs get shingles; the ground blends asphalt, sidewalk and grass by the street mask; lawns are a muted green with dry golden patches (the headlands texture), worn to dirt on steep slopes. Tower windows light up at night where the texture has dark glass.
- `facade.ts`: the older procedural window shader (now only Alcatraz) and `facadeNight`.
- Textures: `public/textures/*.jpg` (about 4.4 MB), generated with Codex from `design/prompts/textures/`.
- `city.tsx`: renders the layout with instanced meshes (a few draw calls for the whole city). The ground is a displaced plane with one canvas texture (`groundTexture` in `textures.ts`); the shore cliffs are an extrusion whose top follows the hills (Telegraph Hill meets the bay as a cliff). Buildings have a footing below ground so slopes never show gaps. Fog is unlit and tinted by time of day.
- `landmarks.tsx`: named places sit on the grid. Signs show the visitor's brand. Landmark buildings use the same facade shader. Detail parts are merged into one geometry per material.
- `parts.tsx`: small shared pieces (`Box`, `useGlow`, `useBrand`).
- `cable-car.tsx`: the double-ended California St car (merged geometry, follows `heightAt`) and its rails with the cable slot. `CAR.step` is the running board Cuso rides at 18:00.
- `pier.tsx`: the 22:00 Embarcadero pier: instanced planks, pilings and bracing, lamp, laptop, mug.
- `traffic.tsx`: 7 cars on two clockwise loops (`LOOPS`), seeded, plus 6 bridge cars: out of the Yerba Buena tunnel or the Marin fog, over the bridge on the right-hand lane, one lap of the downtown or Pacific Heights loop, and back; they turn round only while hidden (inside the tunnel, or past z -282 in the fog). Five one-car-at-a-time spots (three junctions, two tight bends) keep cars apart. Cars keep a gap (per car length) and stop for Cuso, but ignore him when he is more than 1.5 above or below them. One instanced mesh per car model and material (22 draws); wheels are their own instances and roll by the distance each car really drove; a soft contact shadow under each car.
- Car models: `public/models/cars/*.glb`, made by `node scripts/convert-cars.mjs` from the raw files in `3d-assets-raw/` (gitignored). It installs pinned `@gltf-transform/cli` and `occt-import-js` outside the project, sorts parts into named materials, drops logos, normalises size and axes, simplifies each part to a fixed error in metres (keeping creases and material seams), then meshopt-compresses (70 to 120 KB a car). The error (4 mm paint, 5 mm tyres) is a tenth of a pixel in the closest shot; a tighter one only adds triangles nobody sees. Licences of the source models are not confirmed yet (launch blocker).
- `trees.tsx`: four kinds (London plane and ficus on streets, Monterey cypress and palms in parks), clumpy canopies with a leaf texture and baked shading, bark trunks. Two instanced meshes per kind.
- `coit-tower.tsx`: fluted column, observation arches, crown, lobby, on a level stone plaza set into the hill (`coitSeat`). Windows glow at night.
- `bay.tsx`: the water (standard material with a shader patch: ripples, fresnel, sun glint, shallows and shore foam from a distance field baked at load, day to night colour), a planar mirror in full mode, the sailboats (lofted hulls, sail trim from a west wind, heel, wakes), the ferry, Alcatraz and Treasure Island. Boat routes are functions of time and tested to stay on water.
- `roads.ts`: the bridge decks and the four bridge-end roads (`ROADS`, `DECKS`), pure data and geometry, drawn by `Roads()` in `landmarks.tsx` (asphalt, lane marks, concrete: 3 draw calls). Every road starts level with its deck (`DECK_Y` 7), is as wide as the deck and a street (3), keeps the deck's dashed centre line in phase, and never gets steeper than `MAX_GRADE` (0.32). A road's profile is the latest descent its grades allow, rounded over 3 units (`descend`). Where it is high it is a slab on piers (never in a junction or on a cross street); where it is low, retaining walls down to the ground; parapets only where the ground beside it falls away. The asphalt uses the street texture's world mapping, so a road and the street it lands on match. The ends: Golden Gate south over its anchorage, round a bend over the shore onto the Marina street (z -36) at the x -67 junction; north on `MARIN_ROAD` (terrain.ts, cut and fill) over the ridges and out into the fog; Bay Bridge west down its side span (`bayDeckY`) to the SF anchorage at the sea wall, then a viaduct along z 14 that lands at the x 13 junction (east of the Union Square rails); east down its side span into the Yerba Buena tunnel portal. `ISLAND` copies the island dome in `bay.tsx` (tested to match).
- `atmosphere.tsx`: `lightingAt(hour)` every frame; also drives `facadeNight`.
- Post effects (full mode): bloom, tilt shift, a little saturation, vignette, ACES. Phones and weak machines get "lite": no mirror on the water, no tilt shift, 2x multisampling instead of 4x (thin car trim needs it). Check frame rate on a real phone.
- Performance: after the first frame, `Ready` in `scene.tsx` uploads every texture and compiles every shader (`compileAsync`, into a render target because the post effects draw into one and shaders differ by output) while the poster still shows. Production skips the shader error log (reading it stalls on each compile). So the first scroll through a chapter does not hitch. `PerformanceMonitor` drops the pixel ratio to 1 if the frame rate falls.

## Gotchas
- Sun azimuth: 0 = south, 90 = east, 180 = north.
- Screenshots in headless Chrome: it pauses requestAnimationFrame without input, so scripted scrolls do not update the scene. Move the mouse while waiting (see `docs/VERIFY.md`).
- Facade hash: only hash exact per-cell values. Interpolated normals or wall-plane coordinates make windows flicker per pixel (the hash amplifies tiny errors).
- `react-hooks/immutability` lint rule is off in `src/scene/**`: R3F mutates three.js objects in `useFrame` by design.
- three r186 removed soft PCF shadows; we use `shadows="percentage"`.
- Do not add `BrightnessContrast` to the post chain: on a real GPU it turned the near half of the Golden Gate black (found by toggling effects one by one; likely negative colour values before tone mapping). Headless Chrome does not show it.
- A negative number raised to a fractional power in geometry code gives NaN, and one NaN vertex blanks the whole screen through bloom. `bay.test.ts` checks every bay mesh for finite values.
- The water finds the sky dome by how `atmosphere.tsx` builds it (drawn first, inside-facing, uniforms `top` and `horizon`). If that changes, the water falls back to fog colours and the sky shows up in the mirror again.
- Close-ups for art work: in dev, `?cam=px,py,pz,tx,ty,tz` pins the camera (see `camera-rig.tsx`). It is ignored in production.
