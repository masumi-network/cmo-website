# F02 · City scene

Last updated: 2026-10-15 · Status: v0 (art passes 1 to 9 done) · Launch blocker: real-phone performance, final polish

## What it does
- A miniature San Francisco in three.js (React Three Fiber).
- Time of day drives the sun, sky, fog, window lights, street lamps and bridge lights.
- Hills: Russian Hill (under the 06:00 rooftop), Nob Hill, Telegraph Hill, Twin Peaks (two peaks), Pacific Heights. Streets climb them straight up, never steeper than 0.36.
- Parks: Golden Gate Park, Twin Peaks, Pioneer Park on Telegraph Hill (Coit Tower), Dolores Park, with trails, 3D grass, brush and rocks. Grass only grows in parks.
- Street grid with raised sidewalks (real kerbs), crosswalks, SF acorn lamps, hydrants and street signs, and parks. Pastel Victorian row houses modelled in 3D in six variants (bays, cornices, brackets, porches, stoops, flat or gable roofs), ten paint schemes. Downtown in 1920s stone towers, glass towers and SoMa lofts.
- Life: 13 Teslas (10 sedans and SUVs, 2 Cybertrucks, 1 Roadster): 7 on three city loops, 6 crossing the Golden Gate and the Bay Bridge, sailboats with wakes and a ferry on the bay, Alcatraz with its lighthouse.
- Landmarks: Russian Hill rooftop, Hayes Valley shop, Mission wall, Union Square billboard, Transamerica Pyramid, Coit Tower, Ferry Building, California St and Union Square cable cars, Embarcadero pier, Bay Bridge, Golden Gate, and a fog bank off the ocean.

## How it works
- `src/lib/terrain.ts`: `heightAt(x, z)`, a sum of smooth elliptical domes that are exactly flat outside their radius. Everything on the ground uses it, including chapter spots (`at()` in `day.ts`). Hill sizes keep every street under `MAX_STREET_GRADE` (tested); the steep tops sit in parks.
- `grid.ts`: streets, `PARKS` (whole blocks) and `groundAt(x, z)` (street, sidewalk, grass or block). A park closes the streets inside it: the street ends at the park's kerb line and the park's sidewalk runs across it (a T junction). `KERB` (0.14) is how far streets sit below the sidewalks. `LANE_MARKS` (stop 2.7 short of every junction) and `CROSSWALKS` (zebras at the junctions of Cuso's walking streets only) feed the ground mask.
- `terrain.ts`: `heightAt` is the sidewalk and block level; `surfaceAt` is the real ground (streets `KERB` lower, curb ramps where a crosswalk lands). Everything on a street (cars, cable car and rails, bridge-end roads, Cuso when he crosses, chapter spots through `at()`) stands on `surfaceAt`; buildings, trees, lamps and landmarks on `heightAt`. Cuso crosses junctions flush on the ramps and hops the kerb mid-block.
- `ground.ts`: the terrain mesh, a grid every half unit where each kerb line is doubled (one copy at sidewalk height, one at street height) so the kerb is a crisp step, plus one kerb mesh (concrete face, lighter top edge, darker gutter). Cells wholly under the bay are skipped (the water is opaque).
- `layout.ts`: pure and seeded. Also the camera's obstacles: every building box with 0.35 of air, the Pyramid's tapered body and wings (`PYRAMID`, `pyramidHalf`, `WINGS` live here), the Ferry Building (`FERRY`), and the bridges' solids: the Bay Bridge's anchorages, tower piers and steel legs, and every road's piers, each as wide as it really is (the camera passed through the west anchorage on the way to the 22:00 pier); `insideBuilding` checks them. The camera rig keeps the chase camera, the blend between shots and the eased path clear of them. Blocks, houses, towers, trees, lamps. Buildings sit level with their highest corner (nothing sunk); the footing covers the drop on the downhill side. It keeps streets, landmarks (`CLEAR`) and every camera's line of sight (`blocksView`) free. Nothing near the pyramid grows taller than it.
- `house-models.ts` and `houses.tsx`: the row houses, modelled. Six front models (one per variant, measured from the Painted Lady photos: 0 blue-style bay right with columned porch, 1 three storeys bay left, 2 bay on corbels with a tall stoop, 3 dentil cornice and arched door, 4 bay over a garage, 5 three storeys with pediment windows) and two shared bodies (flat roof with a chimney behind the false front, or shingled gable with a fish-scale gable end), as instances scaled uniformly by the plot width. One material (`houseMaterial`): each face carries a paint slot, each pane a window id; `paletteOf` gives every house one of ten paint schemes (`PALETTES`), never the same as the two houses before it in its row. Glass fakes the sky by fresnel (horizon is the hour's fog colour), with blinds and side curtains on some panes. Night: about half the windows light up as whole windows. Fronts stand out up to `RELIEF_DEPTH` (0.36); beside Cuso's walking lines they are flattened (`reliefDepth`) so his arms clear them. Houses are 3.8 to 5.0 tall (`FACADE_ASPECT`).
- `materials.ts`: shader patches on `MeshStandardMaterial`. `houseMaterial` (paint slots, siding, shingles, fish-scale, glass, night windows); `downtownMaterial` for the towers; `downtownMaterial` also draws the Pyramid (part 5 is its aluminium crown; `downtownMaterial(stone, false)` sets its `uCurtain` uniform to 0, so its glass keeps the plain look with no curtain wall detail, same program); `towerMaterial` (limestone landmark walls); `groundMaterial` blends asphalt, sidewalk, block ground and lawn by the street mask (the lawn comes from `lawn.ts`), and darkens where buildings meet the ground (`contactRects` in textures.ts: blurred house, tower and landmark footprints in the green of `lampLight`, up to 0.45; `CONTACT`: 0.25 past the walls, then a 0.6 fade; city.tsx adds the shop, Union Square, the Mission building, the rooftop and the Ferry plinth). `patch()` takes an optional `weather` (weathering.ts); `houseMaterial` and `downtownMaterial` opt in, with their own `uWeather` (tone amount), and keep their program keys.
- `weathering.ts`: real-city wear in the building shaders, after each material's own colour. Grime splashed up from the street: up to `MAX_GRIME` (0.5) darker at the ground, gone by `GRIME_TOP` (1.6). A darker foot line on every wall (`FOOT`: 0.2 high, 0.3 of the grime), then a soft band whose top edge wanders along the wall, in stains that come and go, dirtier on some buildings than others. Lit walls sit in the tone curve's shoulder, so 0.3 at the ground did not show at the chapter cameras. The ground level comes from `GROUND_GLSL`, built from `HILLS` and `PLAZA` (terrain.ts, tested to match `heightAt`). Also big soft patches and faint streaks down the walls, a tone per building (`TONE`: houses ±2%, keeping their curated palettes; towers ±4%), undersides 25% darker (the shade AO gave under cornices, bays and sills) and a little dust on roofs. Glass stays clean. World position only, so the water mirror and the glass probe match. Fine noise fades to its average once a pixel covers it. Every GLSL symbol starts with w (`weathering.test.ts`).
- `facade.ts`: `facadeNight` (the night level `atmosphere.tsx` drives) and the small-scale facade detail `downtownMaterial` calls (`FACADE_GLSL`, every symbol fac-prefixed, no textures beyond stone.jpg). Masonry: limestone ashlar (`ASHLAR`, 0.34 courses of 0.72 blocks, staggered) or running-bond brick (`BRICK`, 0.085 by 0.21) when the tint is red (`isBrick`: linear blue under 0.45 of red); each block takes its grain from a joint-free window of the stone texture and a calm tone of its own; joints fade to their average before they alias, the average stays the tint, and up close the joints bend the normal into a groove. Punched panes: sash frames dark bronze or painted white per tower (steel ones near black or dark green), glass neutral, green or bronze per tower (`glassTint`), the reveal shading the top and one side, blinds in three colours and curtains drawn to the sides. Curtain walls: a hair of tint per pane, shadow-box spandrels, and each pane bows a little, the same in every pane (`OIL_CANNING`: pillow 0.006), in its reflection and sun glint. No lean per pane, no plenum band and no weaker panes: each made the 12:00 tower's reflection hazy (see Glass haze at 12:00). The Pyramid turns all of this off (`uCurtain` 0). `facade.test.ts`.
- `reflections.ts`: real reflections on all glass. One cube probe renders the actual scene over the chapter's camera target (sky, fog, buildings, bay) and glass samples it with box projection (±12 around the probe) and fresnel. It ignores anything nearer than 4 (Cuso, his roof, the billboard). It renders one face per frame into a second buffer and swaps, only after load, on a chapter or mode change and while the hour moves (full: at most every 0.25 s; lite: 128 px, once per chapter). `scene.environment` stays the studio light; only glass uses the probe. The shop window glass (`glassMaterial` in shop.tsx, also used by the Ferry arcade, Union Square and the taqueria) is nearly clear head-on and reflects at glancing angles.
- Textures: `public/textures/*.jpg`, generated with Codex from `design/prompts/textures/`. The facade photo atlas and the glass texture are gone (houses and towers are modelled now).
- `city.tsx`: renders the layout (a few draw calls for the whole city), the shore sea wall (lowered where a street meets it). Fog lives in `fog.tsx`.
- Marin Headlands (`headlandHeight` in terrain.ts): every edge of the mesh slopes into the sea (`coasts()`: Point Bonita on the west, the bay toward Sausalito on the east, a north coast past the end of the Marin road over 44 units, `NORTH_COAST`); bounds are `HEADLANDS`. A cut edge used to show in the water's mirror as a curved light edge left of the Golden Gate's south tower. The water's shore field (`SHORE` in bay.tsx) covers the whole headlands. The strait shore (`marinShore`) runs in coves and small points, and the hills rise from it over 22 to 46 units (`riseWidth`): bluffs here, gentle slopes there. Both stay as built within about 20 units of the bridge, so the landing does not move. A straight shore at z -128 read from the bay as the mesh cut off at the water (2026-10-14; `bay.test.ts` checks it).
- `fog.tsx`: Karl the fog as one continuous bank: 14 stacked horizontal layers (10 in lite), cut into 10x10 tiles only where fog reaches, in one mesh with two materials (the tiles near the bridge towers write depth so a tower fades into the fog). Each layer samples the fog up to half a layer along the view ray with a static per-pixel dither, so the stack reads as one smooth volume with no slices; unlit, transparent, on the `DETAIL` layer (kept out of the mirror and the probe). A map baked at load holds the bank's depth: full over the ocean, thinning east through the strait under the Golden Gate deck and along the Marin shore, kept 5 back from the city, and thinned in a wedge straight behind Cuso in the 06:00 shot. Low-frequency noise gives rolling soft mounds on top, drifting east; it fades softly onto water and land and stays under the deck. Tinted by the continuous hour (pale, warm at sunset, blue-grey at night). Hidden in explore. `fog.test.ts` keeps it off every chapter spot and camera.
- `towers.tsx`: downtown as two merged meshes: the body (massing, crowns, rooftop tanks; casts shadows) and the relief (window depth; receives only). Each tower gets a style from its own data (`towerStyle`): stone 1920s (granite shop base, shaft set 0.2 back over it, stone piers, glass set 0.1 back with sills, heads and sash panes, belt courses over the base and under the top storey, a slate pyramid, stepped or flat crown with a water tank), glass (curtain wall with proud metal fins and spandrel bands, glazed lobby, sloped top or parapet) or a SoMa loft (brick or concrete piers, steel factory windows on a sill course, shops below). Shop fronts sit 0.12 back between piers. Cornices come from `corniceProfile`: stacked steps whose top step (the corona) projects most, at the roof line: 0.2 on stone crowns (frieze, bed, modillions, corona, parapet), 0.17 at setbacks, 0.16 on lofts (brick course on corbels, concrete cornice, brick parapet with a coping). Cornice steps are bands (`shape: "band"`, only their visible faces) in the body, so they cast shadows; belts, modillions and corbels are `fine` and go to the relief. Windows stop under the cornice. Tests keep cornices inside the footprint, belts between a head and the sill above, and no two differently shaded faces in one plane (z-fighting). Rooftop parts stay within `ROOF_MARGIN` (1) and under the Pyramid limit. Two stone crowns are floodlit at night.
- `lawn.ts` and `grass.tsx`: lawns. Colour at several scales (gold on sunny wild slopes, green in mown gardens and shade, darker by the kerb), trails (dirt on the wild hills, gravel in the gardens), gullies down from the summits. 3D grass: one instanced draw of tufts that take the lawn colour under them and fade out with distance (20 to 34 units, 12 to 20 in lite), wind in the vertex shader, no shadows cast. Coyote brush and rock outcrops on Twin Peaks and Telegraph Hill. Nothing grows on streets, trails, Coit's plaza, under trees or on Cuso's routes.
- `street.tsx`: street furniture, placed by `streetFurniture()` in layout.ts (seeded): 43 SF post-top acorn lamps on two opposite corners of each junction (none on walking-street corners, which are curb ramps), 17 white hydrants with a blue bonnet and 7 green street-name signs at walking-street junctions. All stand on flat sidewalk 0.3 in from the kerb, off ramps, trees, bridge roads, chapter views and more than 1.2 from Cuso's routes (`street.test.ts`; `collision.test.ts` counts them as solids). At night each lamp paints a light pool into the ground shader (`lampLight` in textures.ts, no extra draw). Road paint is matte off-white (about 0.6, under the bloom threshold) with worn patches and its own share of the mask, so no grey halo.
- `landmarks.tsx`: both bridges and the bridge-end roads. Shared helpers (merged parts, landmark textures, materials) are in `landmark-kit.tsx`.
- `mission.tsx`: the 09:00 Mission corner building (house kit, apricot scheme): a taqueria with an awning and sign band, flats with bays above, a flat stucco side wall carrying the 4 x 3 post grid (a new post appears when you approve), lamps washing the wall at night, a wooden ladder and a paint pail on the sidewalk. Behind clear shop glass a small taqueria: Saltillo tile, ochre wainscot, a lit menu board, a salsa bar, a drinks cooler, papel picado, tables with bentwood chairs; light baked in the shader.
- `union-square.tsx`: the 12:00 stop. An early-1900s commercial block (house kit, cream scheme): two-storey shop base with lit display showcases behind clear glass (four rotating themes, muted colours, spotlights) and a lobby at the corner entrance, Chicago windows above, cornice, a roof with a parapet on three sides (none on the south, where Cuso jumps up). The billboard is centred on the roof (`board.x` from the footprint): steel truss, catwalk, gooseneck lamps, the visitor's ad (`AD_PANEL`). The Dewey Monument stands in the square west of it.
- `ferry.tsx`: the 18:00 Ferry Building (house kit, cream scheme, copper roof slot): an arcade of round arches with glass set back, arched windows and attic windows, brackets and cornice, a copper hipped roof with a long skylight, and the Giralda-style clock tower (shaft, clock stage with four dials, two open belfries, a domed lantern). Behind the arcade, through clear glass, a marketplace: produce, bakery, flowers, cheese and coffee stalls in turn, named shopfronts, pendants; light baked in the shader. The passage under the tower is closed by lit doorways. Also the California St cable car and the newsletter envelopes.
- `islands.tsx`: Alcatraz (layered cliffs with benches, a switchback road from the quay, the cellhouse with barred windows and roof monitors, the lighthouse whose lantern is the beam's pivot, the water tower, the warden's house ruin, the yard, Building 64 on its pier) and Treasure Island (three ribbed hangars with clerestories, Art Moderne Building One, a palm avenue). The water mirror reflects both.
- `rooftop.tsx`: the 06:00 Russian Hill apartment, modelled with the row houses' kit and `houseMaterial` (cream scheme): stone base with the front door at x −14, two angled bays per wall on corbels, belt courses, a modillion cornice exactly on the collision ring (`ROOFTOP.cornice`), tar and gravel deck, coping, stair bulkhead with a door. One draw call. `ROOFTOP` is unchanged (Cuso's jump and the collision solids read it).
- `shop.tsx`: the 07:00 Hayes Valley coffee shop. A two-storey corner building (house kit, cream scheme) over a modelled timber shopfront in deep green: pilasters, stall risers, display glass with transoms, a recessed door, the brand on the fascia (`SHOP_SIGN`, `useSign`). Behind clear glass a crafted café (reference: `design/prompts/cafe-interior.jpg`): bentwood chairs, marble tables on cast-iron bases, a tufted leather banquette, stools, a chrome espresso machine, grinder and pastry case, subway tile, beadboard wainscot, a pressed-tin ceiling, framed prints, shelves of coffee bags and a chalk menu both carrying the visitor's brand, enamel pendants. Light is computed in its shader: pendant pools, under-shelf strips, contact shadows, a morning sun shaft cut by the mullions; one draw call, no real-time lights. Floor level with the top of its front sidewalk. Outside, the shopfront weathers like the buildings (`weather()` from weathering.ts in its own shader). Bistro table and A-frame on the sidewalk; the corner lamp is the city's acorn lamp (`Lamps` in street.tsx).
- `pyramid.tsx`: the 15:00 Transamerica Pyramid as one merged mesh (`pyramidGeometry`): 38 storeys of precast floor bands and mullion ribs with glass set back, stepped corners, the A-frame arcade and recessed lobby at the base, plumb wings (`WINGS`), an aluminium crown and spire, a red beacon (`BEACON`). It stands on a level plaza cut into the hill (`PLAZA` in terrain.ts) with retaining walls on three sides. `PYRAMID`, `SPIRE`, `FLAG` and the platform height are unchanged.
- `parts.tsx`: small shared pieces (`Box`, `useGlow`, `useBrand`).
- `cable-car.tsx`: the double-ended cable car model (`CarModel`, merged geometry, follows `surfaceAt`) and its rails with the cable slot. `CAR.step` is the running board Cuso rides at 18:00 on the California St car (parked).
- `cable-line.ts`: the Union Square car runs its line on z 14, rails x -8.3 to 10.5 (`CABLE_RAILS` in grid.ts: no centre line between the rails, crosswalk bars spaced so the rails and slot sit in the gaps). Stops: west end (x -6.5), Union Square (2.1, `UNION_CAR.p`) and east end (8.7); it takes the cable, cruises at 1.6, brakes into each stop, dwells and reverses at the ends (double-ended, no turntable: no calm place for one). It yields to Cuso: the stretches of his routes where he could touch it are worked out once, the car only commits to one when he is clear and otherwise brakes short, and Cuso waits at the kerb for a car that has committed (`walkOn` in routes.ts). While 07:00 or 12:00 is the active chapter it returns to Union Square and waits there; it runs between chapters and in explore. Kept out of reflections like traffic. `cable-line.test.ts` and the time-aware cases in `collision.test.ts` check it.
- `pier.tsx`: the 22:00 Embarcadero pier: instanced planks, pilings and bracing, lamp, laptop, mug.
- `traffic.tsx`: 13 Teslas. 7 drive three clockwise loops (`LOOPS`, corners listed clockwise): 5 round the downtown block, 1 round Pacific Heights, and the Roadster alone on a long L through North Beach, down x -19 to the Mission, west under Twin Peaks, up the Sunset to Golden Gate Park and north up x -35 (six corners, one of them a left turn). 6 bridge cars come out of the Yerba Buena tunnel or the Marin fog, cross on the right-hand lane, do one lap of the downtown or Pacific Heights loop and go back; they turn round only while hidden (inside the tunnel, or past z -282 in the fog). Turns: a route is the path of a car's rear axle and the car points the way it runs there, so the nose swings wide and the rear cuts in, like a real car. Each square corner is one `turn()`: the steering winds on and off as half cosines (no sudden yaw), right turns in 0.8 and out over 2, left turns gently over 4.8 each way and out 0.6 short of the lane, over the centre line, drifting back over 12. On the bridge roads the pivot moves to the car's middle (eased over 6 units): their lanes are as narrow as the cars, and a swinging nose would brush the oncoming lane in their long bends. Road lanes are rounded (Chaikin) so the heading turns smoothly. Speed: each route has a pace per point (`GRIP` 1.2 sideways, `BRAKE` 1.6, `PULL` 1.2): a car slows to about 1.1 into a tight corner and pulls away after it; its loop speed (2.3 to 3.2) is the cap. Ten one-car-at-a-time zones (junctions where routes meet, the drifts after left turns on the Bay ramp and the Marina street, two tight bends) keep cars apart; the zones where the Roadster meets the other loops are wide (6.5), so a car waiting to go in stands clear of a nose swinging round the corner. Cars keep a gap (per car length) and stop for Cuso, but ignore him when he is more than 1.5 above or below them. One instanced mesh per car model and material (22 draws); wheels are their own instances and roll by the distance each car really drove; a soft contact shadow under each car.
- Car models: four Teslas, `public/models/cars/{model3,modely,cybertruck,roadster}.glb` plus a lite set in `public/models/cars/lite/`, made by `node scripts/convert-cars.mjs` (and `--lite`) from the Sketchfab glTF zips in `3d-assets-raw/tesla/` (gitignored; Model 3 Highland, Model Y Juniper, production Cybertruck, Roadster 2020). It installs pinned `@gltf-transform` tools outside the project, reads each glTF in world space, drops the interior and hidden parts, sorts materials into seven parts (paint, glass, tyre, rim, front and rear lights, trim), keeps one wheel (snapped to the ground, smooth tyres), normalises size and axes, simplifies to a fixed error (4 mm paint and glass; lite looser, seams unlocked) and meshopt-compresses. Full 4.6k-11k triangles drawn per car (44-74 KB), lite 32-57% of that. Mix: 5 Model 3, 5 Model Y, 2 Cybertrucks, 1 Roadster, scaled 0.68 (Cybertruck 0.62). Up to 29 instanced draws (a model only draws when one of its cars is in view). The cars stay Teslas (San Francisco is full of them); the site is not commercial, so model licences are not a blocker and there is no credits line (decided 2026-10-14).
- `tree-models.ts`, `tree-textures.ts` and `trees.tsx`: four kinds grown from seeds (3 variants each): London plane (mottled peeling bark, open lumpy crown), ficus (smooth grey trunk, dense glossy dome), Monterey cypress (gnarled, leaning east, flat foliage pads), Canary palm (diamond leaf-base trunk, 26 arching fronds). Leaves are alpha-tested cards from a canvas leaf atlas, shaded as one soft mass, darker inside, a little backlight, wind gusts from the west. Two batched draws for every tree (multi-draw); beyond 26 units a tree switches to its far model (lite always uses it). Street trees stand in a cast-iron grate.
- `coit-tower.tsx`: Coit Tower on Pioneer Park's level plaza (`coitSeat`), one solid mesh plus glass. The fluted shaft on a stepped base course with pour lines and rain streaks; an open observation arcade of 20 arches with depth (lit inside at night), blind arched windows and a stepped cornice above. A low Art Deco base building with rounded corners, tall stepped window recesses and the west entrance; the WPA murals show through the glass and the door. The plaza: paving in rings, a coursed retaining wall, a coped parapet with piers, acorn lamps and benches facing the view. At night the shaft is floodlit from below. The Columbus statue was left out: it was removed in 2020.
- `bay.tsx`: the water (standard material with a shader patch: ripples, fresnel, sun glint, shallows and shore foam from a distance field baked at load, day to night colour), a planar mirror in full mode, the sailboats (lofted hulls, sail trim from a west wind, heel, wakes), the ferry, Alcatraz and Treasure Island. Boat routes are functions of time and tested to stay on water.
- `roads.ts`: the bridge decks and the four bridge-end roads (`ROADS`, `DECKS`), pure data and geometry, drawn by `Roads()` in `landmarks.tsx` (asphalt, lane marks, concrete: 3 draw calls). Every road starts level with its deck (`DECK_Y` 7), is as wide as the deck and a street (3), keeps the deck's dashed centre line in phase, and never gets steeper than `MAX_GRADE` (0.32). A road's profile is the latest descent its grades allow, rounded over 3 units (`descend`). Where it is high it is a slab on piers (never in a junction or on a cross street); where it is low, retaining walls down to the ground; parapets only where the ground beside it falls away. The asphalt uses the street texture's world mapping, so a road and the street it lands on match. The ends: Golden Gate south over its anchorage, round a bend over the shore onto the Marina street (z -36) at the x -67 junction; north on `MARIN_ROAD` (terrain.ts, cut and fill) over the ridges and out into the fog; Bay Bridge west down its side span (`bayDeckY`) to the SF anchorage at the sea wall, then a viaduct along z 14 that lands at the x 13 junction (east of the Union Square rails); east down its side span into the Yerba Buena tunnel portal. `ISLAND` copies the island dome in `bay.tsx` (tested to match).
- Bridge masonry (`landmarks.tsx`, `roads.ts`): one `concreteMaterial` (pour lines, panel joints, form-tie dots up close, rain streaks under ledges from the `aTop` attribute, grime and a wet band at the waterline). Golden Gate: Art Deco anchorages with pilasters and recessed panels (`ggMasonry`, under `GG.anchorTop`), concrete pylons with stepped fluting beside the road (`GG_PYLON`), octagonal fender piers, and the Fort Point arch in orange steel over the street at the city's tip (`ggArch`). Bay Bridge: anchorages with tall pilasters (`bayMasonry`) and lower chamfered tower piers, so the steel legs show. Approach piers taper into a hammerhead under the slab; parapets have a coping. The Yerba Buena tunnel has an arched portal with stepped rings and lamps inside (`PORTAL`).
- `atmosphere.tsx`: `lightingAt(hour)` every frame; also drives `facadeNight`. Distance fog (`fogRange`): 70 to 340 in the story, 260 to 900 in Explore; after an Explore pin it eases in by the camera's distance from what it looks at (50 to 120), since it switched with the mode and hid the city in a white haze while the camera was still high. Karl the fog bank (`fog.tsx`, hidden in Explore) fades in by the same factor (`overhead`), not at full strength beside the city. `atmosphere.test.ts`.
- Post effects (`post.tsx`, full mode): bloom, tilt shift, a little saturation, vignette, ACES, and a colour grade with grain. They follow a per-hour look (`src/lib/look.ts`, F13 [photo-look](photo-look.md)): at 12:00 depth of field on Cuso replaces the tilt shift; every other hour is today's look. Multisampling 2x on retina screens (looks like 4x on the bridge cables, about 1.2 ms cheaper), 4x at pixel ratio 1. Phones and weak machines get "lite" (`experience.tsx`: touch phones, `?lite`, `deviceMemory` of 4 or less, or an Intel, Mali, Adreno or PowerVR GPU): no mirror on the water, no tilt shift or depth of field (grade and grain stay), no car, rock or shrub shadows (the soft blob under each car stays), a 1024 shadow map, coarser Marin hills, fewer grass blades, far tree models, a 128 px glass probe.
- Performance: after the first frame, `Ready` in `scene.tsx` uploads every texture, compiles every shader (`compileAsync`, into a render target because the post effects draw into one) and then renders the whole scene once into a 1x1 target with culling off and hidden objects shown (`warmUp`), which also compiles the shadow shaders `compileAsync` never covers. The water mirror and the glass probe wait for it (`shadersReady`). So nothing compiles after the poster fades. The studio `<Environment>` and `Scene` are memoised (a chapter change used to re-render and re-filter the cube). Small details sit on the `DETAIL` layer (`shared.ts`): the main camera draws them, the mirror and the probe do not (grass, rocks, shrubs, hydrants, signs, kerbs, cars, interiors). Interiors are hidden when the camera is more than 40 past them (`useInterior`). Houses are merged by 40-unit district so each culls on its own. Marin and car glass cast no shadows. The camera rig and the bubble measure the overlays once per scroll position (`overlays()`), not every frame. `PerformanceMonitor` drops the pixel ratio to 1 if the frame rate falls. In dev, `?perf` shows fps, draw calls, triangles, programs and pixel ratio (`perf-overlay.tsx`, not in production). `tests/e2e/perf.spec.ts` (`@heavy`) keeps draw calls and triangles per chapter under ceilings, desktop full and Pixel 7 lite. GPU time per frame on the real GPU: `CHAPTERS=ads node scripts/frame-bench.mjs [label]` with the dev server running (`scripts/frame-bench.mjs`: median of `FRAMES`, default 120, at each chapter's rest view, 1440x900, EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2; no `CHAPTERS` means every chapter but join).

## Acceptance

## Gotchas
- Sun azimuth: 0 = south, 90 = east, 180 = north.
- Screenshots in headless Chrome: it pauses requestAnimationFrame without input, so scripted scrolls do not update the scene. Move the mouse while waiting (see `docs/VERIFY.md`).
- Facade hash: only hash exact per-cell values. Interpolated normals or wall-plane coordinates make windows flicker per pixel (the hash amplifies tiny errors). Never hash a value that sits exactly on a `floor()` step (e.g. `floor(n * 1.5 + 0.5)` for a normal of ±1 lands on -1.0 or 2.0): use `floor(n + 0.5)` and flat varyings. Desktop Metal hides it; the software renderer the e2e runs on (SwiftShader) shows it as white speckle on every window.
- `react-hooks/immutability` lint rule is off in `src/scene/**`: R3F mutates three.js objects in `useFrame` by design.
- three r186 removed soft PCF shadows; we use `shadows="percentage"`.
- Do not add `BrightnessContrast` to the post chain: on a real GPU it turned the near half of the Golden Gate black (found by toggling effects one by one; likely negative colour values before tone mapping). Headless Chrome does not show it.
- A negative number raised to a fractional power in geometry code gives NaN, and one NaN vertex blanks the whole screen through bloom. `bay.test.ts` checks every bay mesh for finite values.
- The water finds the sky dome by how `atmosphere.tsx` builds it (drawn first, inside-facing, uniforms `top` and `horizon`). If that changes, the water falls back to fog colours and the sky shows up in the mirror again.
- Close-ups for art work: in dev, `?cam=px,py,pz,tx,ty,tz` pins the camera (see `camera-rig.tsx`). It is ignored in production.
